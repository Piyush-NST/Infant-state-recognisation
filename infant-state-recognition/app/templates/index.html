Project Objective

Infants communicate their needs and physical discomforts primarily through crying. This system utilizes advanced Machine Learning and Deep Learning architectures to recognize distinct infant states from acoustic signals, offering parents and healthcare providers automated, real-time insights.

By classifying baby cries into specific behavioral categories, we bridge the gap between neonatal vocalizations and actionable care actions.

Core Specs & Classes

The system classifies input signals into 11 distinct classes corresponding to baby crying triggers and baseline states:

  • Hunger, Discomfort, Burping, Silence, Noise
  • Belly Pain, Lonely, Scared, Tired, Laugh, Cold/Hot
Step 1

Audio Acquisition

User uploads baby cry audio clips (WAV, MP3, OGG, FLAC, M4A) which are safely archived in the system's database.

Step 2

Preprocessing Pipeline

Acoustic signals undergo resampling, amplitude normalization, silence trimming, and noise filtering to isolate cry triggers.

Step 3

Feature Engineering

Extracts Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), Chroma vectors, Spectral Contrast, Spectral Rolloff, RMS, and 2D Mel-Spectrogram frames.

Step 4

Model Inference Zoo

Processes extracted feature inputs through a series of model classifiers (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, SVM, XGBoost, and MobileNetV2 Neural Network) to return classification categories and prediction probabilities.

Model Performance Benchmarks

This comparison evaluates the validation accuracy and F1-scores across all classical Machine Learning architectures and the Deep Learning MobileNetV2 network. The Advanced SVM and Random Forest models demonstrate exceptional accuracy on the engineered 142-dimensional feature space.

Acoustic Feature Importance

Analysis of feature coefficients shows the top acoustic properties driving the XGBoost classifier. Lower-order Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), spectral roll-off frequencies, and RMS energy levels constitute the most predictive signals.

Deep Learning Training History

Shows the loss and accuracy trajectories for the MobileNetV2 architecture. The model stabilizes after approximately 30 epochs of training on the mel-spectrogram arrays, achieving balanced generalization.
